[~1:10] Now the results. First, the simulated test set: four thousand chains from proteins the model never saw. The parity plot is tight along the diagonal. The mean absolute error is 0.10 nanometres, and R squared is 0.98. But we want to be careful here, because length alone explains a lot of Rg. So we compare with the simplest baseline from our proposal: a power law in length, fitted on the training set. That baseline already gets R squared 0.97 — so a high R squared by itself does not prove much. Against that baseline, the graph model cuts the mean absolute error by about nineteen percent, from 0.124 to 0.100 nanometres. A better way to see it: if we remove what length explains and look only at what is left — how much more compact or expanded a chain is than its length predicts — the graph model captures about thirty-six percent of that leftover variance. That leftover part is exactly the sequence effect we care about.
