[~1:00] Let’s start with the biology. The textbook picture is that a protein folds into one 3D structure, and that structure decides its function. On the left is that picture: the energy landscape has one deep well, so the protein spends almost all of its time in one shape. But roughly thirty percent of the eukaryotic proteome is intrinsically disordered — whole proteins, or long regions of them, that never fold. We call them IDPs or IDRs. Their landscape, on the right, is shallow and flat. There are many small wells and the chain keeps hopping between them. So there is no single structure to predict; what is well defined is the ensemble — the average behaviour over all those shapes. These regions are not junk. They regulate transcription, carry cell signals, help form condensates through phase separation, and act in molecular recognition. So how they behave as an ensemble really matters.
