Method · Step 1–2

Learn a contact prior from 28 million simulation frames

How we count

In every frame, a pair (i, j) with |i − j| ≥ 2 is a contact if closer than 8 Å. Pooled by amino-acid type:

f(a,b) = contacts / opportunities
ε(a,b) = log( f(a,b) / mean f )

28,058 chains → a 12 KB table, built once.

What it learned

Enriched: W–W (+0.48), A–A, Y–Y (+0.35), aromatic stickers.
Depleted: K–P (−0.27), D–P, E–P, proline with charges.

Tian & Chung · COMP 5222

[~1:10] Step one is the contact prior. We went through every frame of every Human IDRome trajectory — about twenty-eight thousand chains and twenty-eight million frames in total. For each pair of residues at least two apart in the sequence, we ask: are they closer than eight angstroms? We then pool these counts by amino-acid type. For each pair type a, b we get a contact frequency f: the number of contacts divided by the number of chances. We also log-normalise it against the average, which gives the enrichment epsilon you see in the heatmap. Red means that pair touches more often than average; blue means less often. The table makes physical sense. The most enriched pairs are aromatic: tryptophan–tryptophan, tyrosine–tyrosine — the “sticker” residues known to drive compaction and phase separation. The most depleted are proline with charged residues like lysine and aspartate. One caution: these are contact tendencies in this dataset, not true interaction energies. And the whole thing is a twelve-kilobyte file, so at inference we never touch a trajectory.
