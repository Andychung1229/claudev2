[~0:55] The harder test is real experimental data: 151 sequences with measured Rg. Remember, the model never saw a single experimental label. It still follows the diagonal, with a Pearson correlation of 0.92 and R squared of 0.85. And here the gain over length alone is larger: the error drops from 0.42 to 0.30 nanometres. We also checked for overlap. Twenty-two of these 151 sequences, the orange diamonds, appear inside a training sequence. If we remove them, the numbers barely change, so the result is not driven by memorisation. The honest caveat: the error is about three times larger than on the simulated test set. Our labels come from one simulation model, so whatever that model gets wrong about real proteins, we inherit. Closing that gap would need experimental data or a better simulation model, not just a better graph.
