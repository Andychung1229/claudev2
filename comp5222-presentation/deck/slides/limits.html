[~0:55] Our edge score divides the pair's contact frequency by the average frequency at that distance. But contact frequency varies only two-fold between amino-acid pairs, while the distance term varies over a thousand-fold. So distance always wins: 97 percent of chosen partners lie in the farthest available distance band. The amino-acid type only breaks ties inside a band. You can see the consequence in the experiments. Here are six sequences with the same composition in different orders: their measured Rg spans 0.7 nanometres, but our predictions span less than 0.1. In other words, our graph barely sees residue order, and order, like charge patterning, is exactly what matters for disordered proteins. That points directly to what we do next.
