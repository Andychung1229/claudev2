[~0:55] Our edge score divides the pair's contact frequency by the average frequency at that distance. Pair frequency varies only two-fold, but the distance term varies over a thousand-fold. So distance wins: 97 percent of chosen partners sit in the farthest distance band, and amino-acid type only breaks ties. A control that picks edges by distance alone behaves the same. The cost shows up in residue order. Here are six sequences with the same composition in different orders. Their measured Rg spans 0.73 nanometres. With contact edges, our predictions span 0.06. Without them, 0.47, almost in the right order. Our guess is that a dozen far-away edges per residue drown out the local signal in attention. For the long chains, no variant sees order yet.
