[~0:45] Here is what a whole graph looks like, for a longer chain of 155 residues. This is the adjacency matrix: blue is the local band along the diagonal, orange are the non-local edges. Only about ten percent of all residue pairs become edges. In general the number of edges grows like N times w plus k, instead of N squared for a complete graph, which keeps message passing cheap. Each edge also carries ten numbers: the contact frequency from our table, the normalised sequence distance, a one-hot code for which distance band it falls in, and two flags for local edges and self-loops. You might notice that the orange edges all sit far from the diagonal. We come back to why in the limitations.
